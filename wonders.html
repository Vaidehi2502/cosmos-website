Astrophysical extremes

Expand each panel to explore objects and phenomena that push matter, space, and time to their absolute edges.

Neutron stars: a teaspoon that weighs a billion tonnes

A neutron star packs more mass than the Sun into a sphere roughly 20 kilometres across. Its matter is so compressed that a single teaspoon would weigh around a billion tonnes on Earth, and its surface gravity is about 200 billion times stronger than ours.

Cosmic voids: the vast emptiness between galaxies

Cosmic voids are enormous regions — some over 300 million light-years wide — containing almost no galaxies. If the Milky Way sat at the centre of the Boötes Void, we might not have discovered other galaxies until the 1960s, because the nearest ones would be unimaginably far away.

Photon latency: starlight is a message from the past

Light travels at about 300,000 kilometres per second, yet space is so vast that the glow you see from distant stars left them long ago. Sunlight takes about 8 minutes and 20 seconds to reach Earth, so you always see the Sun as it was in the past, never as it is right now.
